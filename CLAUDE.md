# Transcribe

On-device conversation transcription for iPhone (iPhone 15 is the target device) and Mac. After a recording ends, the app separates the speakers. A voice shows as "Speaker 1 / Speaker 2" until it is named once; the app then saves a voice profile and auto-labels that person in later recordings.

Status: iPhone and Mac apps in development (fake and FluidAudio engines). On a real iPhone 15 all three speech models load, and the one-off setup after each install or update takes about 20 s (measured 2026-11-25). Optional iCloud sync of transcripts and voice profiles is built and tested against a fake iCloud only; it has never run against real CloudKit (see "Not yet verified"). The Mac app ships outside the App Store as a Developer ID–signed, notarised DMG; the public download is version 0.3.1 (2026-11-25); 0.2.0 was the first with read-only Claude access (MCP). Claude access was tested with the fake engine and Debug builds, including a real Claude Code client going through pairing; nobody has yet used it with the Developer ID build, a real library or Claude Desktop (see "Not yet verified"). In-app updates for the Mac (Sparkle) and their release by CI to Cloudflare R2 work (2026-11-25): from 0.3.0 the Mac app checks w2solutions.ai for new versions and updates itself. CI published 0.3.0 and 0.3.1, and Liam updated 0.3.0 to 0.3.1 in-app in `/Applications`. 0.2.0 cannot, so its users download a newer version once by hand. From 0.3.2 a release also publishes a delta update from the version before (a few MB instead of about 657 MB; see "Mac updates (Sparkle)"). Global shortcuts and dictation on the Mac (Settings → Shortcuts) are built; `swift test` covers their pure logic and `DictationController` with the fake engine, the simulated-shortcut UI tests (`MacShortcutsTests`) compile but have never run. On 2026-11-26 Liam used them in a sandboxed Debug test copy with the real engine: fn Z started and stopped recordings, fn X dictated, and after the guided Accessibility setup it pasted (see "Not yet verified" for the rest). Audio sync (a compressed copy of each finished recording's audio through the same private iCloud, so a recording plays on the other devices; see "Audio sync") is built and tested against a fake iCloud only (2026-11-26): it has never run against real CloudKit or on two real devices, its two record types are not deployed in either CloudKit environment, and no version has been bumped for it (see "Releasing audio sync (0.3.4)"). Recording Mac audio (calls and other apps) on the Mac, and matching voices per sound group, are built on the branch `system-audio-voice-groups` (schema V5, not released) and tested with the fake engine and scripted audio only: the real Core Audio tap has never run (see "Mac system audio" and "Not yet verified").

## Product rules

- All speech processing runs on-device and needs no network: the speech models ship inside the app. The only network use is optional iCloud sync (with Sync audio on, it includes a compressed copy of each finished recording's audio) and, on the Mac, update checks and downloads from w2solutions.ai (no user data: the app version and what any web request carries), and the loopback-only Claude access below. On the Mac, the sound of calls and other apps is recorded only when the user chooses Mac audio; it is kept like any other recording (with Sync audio on, a compressed copy of its audio syncs too).
- Speaker separation runs after the recording finishes, never live. Live text has no speaker labels.
- Naming a speaker once saves a voice profile; matching against saved profiles is automatic afterwards, and the user can correct a wrong match.
- Sync is optional and off by default. Each device has its own "Sync with iCloud" switch (Settings → iCloud). When it is on, transcripts, speaker labels, folders and voice profiles (names, samples, negatives, sample group labels) sync through the user's private iCloud database. Turning sync off never deletes local data. The iCloud copy (audio included) is deleted only when the user chooses "Remove iCloud Copy…", or "Also Remove from iCloud" when turning sync off; when some recordings' audio is only in iCloud, that dialog first says how many and offers Download Audio First.
- Audio syncs as a compressed copy (decided 2026-11-26 at Liam's request; see "Audio sync"). Each device has its own "Sync audio" switch under Settings → iCloud, on by default, which acts only while that device's "Sync with iCloud" is on (still off by default); the switch is always shown, and disabled while sync is off. With both on, the device uploads a compressed copy (AAC, about 15 MB an hour) of the audio of its finished recordings to the user's private iCloud and downloads the audio of recordings made on the other devices, so they play there: player, tappable timestamps, the Ask banner's play disc, voice sample clips, "Share audio", and the Mac's Space key and Playback menu. The Mac downloads every recording's audio by itself; the iPhone downloads the last 30 days by itself and older audio when a recording is opened. Automatic transfers use Wi-Fi or Ethernet only (never mobile data, a personal hotspot or Low Data Mode) and wait in Low Power Mode; Download Now (on the transcript) and Sync Audio Now (Settings) use any network and run in Low Power Mode, and either one interrupts an automatic transfer. Until the audio is here the transcript says why, without a player ("Audio is on your iPhone and isn't in iCloud yet…", "Audio is in iCloud (14 MB). It downloads on Wi-Fi.", "Audio is in iCloud. Turn on Sync audio in Settings to play it here.", "Update Transcribe to play this audio."). Turning sync or Sync audio off deletes nothing, here or in iCloud. Deleting a recording deletes its audio from every device and from iCloud. After the update, a device that already syncs shows a one-time card on the Library that its audio is uploading ("Audio now syncs with iCloud"), with Turn Off and OK; the backlog uploads once, newest first.
- Voices are matched per microphone: every recording, sample and negative carries a capture channel (`iphone`, `ipad`, `mac`), taken from the recording the voice was heard in. A voice learned only on another device is never auto-labelled: it is asked about ("Is this Karan?") until the user confirms it once on this device. Mac audio is not a channel of its own: a call recorded on the Mac is on `mac`, and a voice heard through call audio is the same person in another sound environment, kept apart by sound groups (decision D2, 2026-11-26). A voice heard on both the microphone and Mac audio in one recording is only asked about, and nothing is learned from it in that recording.
- Recording Mac audio (Mac only, decided 2026-11-26): Settings → Recording → Record: Microphone (the default), Microphone and Mac audio, or Mac audio only, remembered per Mac and never synced. The toolbar's New Recording menu and File → Record From choose it too (and change the setting); every source control is disabled while recording. Before the first Mac audio recording an explainer sheet says macOS will ask under Screen & System Audio Recording, that Transcribe records sound only, never the screen, and keeps it with the recording like the microphone's audio (not that it stays on the Mac: with Sync audio on it syncs). The recording window names its source; with both it shows two meters (Microphone, Mac audio), a headphones hint while the Mac plays through its own speakers, and a note when Mac audio stays silent (with Open Privacy Settings) or stops (the microphone carries on). The iPhone records the microphone only and shows no source control.
- Claude access is optional, Mac only, off by default and read-only (Settings → Claude). When it is on, Claude Code and Claude Desktop on that Mac can read transcripts, titles, folders and speaker names of finished recordings (never audio or voice data), once the user has allowed each Claude app in Transcribe (an Allow prompt with a code that Claude also shows); allowed apps can be removed in Settings → Claude. What Claude reads goes to Anthropic. Besides this loopback-only connection, the only network use is optional iCloud sync (audio included, with Sync audio on) and the Mac's update checks and downloads from w2solutions.ai.
- Global shortcuts and dictation (Mac only). Three shortcuts work from any app while Transcribe is open. Each can be changed or set to Off in Settings → Shortcuts (also the app menu's Shortcuts…), and they are stored per Mac, never synced:
  - "Start or stop a recording" (default fn Z) does what the record button does. It never brings Transcribe forward or opens a window while another app is in front; a short notice (the HUD) and a "●" Dock badge show instead.
  - "Dictate (hold to talk)" (default fn X): hold, speak, let go to paste. A tap under 0.3 s only shows a hint.
  - "Dictate hands-free" (default fn ⇧ X): press to start, press again to stop. Esc cancels; after 10 s, Esc must be pressed twice.

  Dictation is transcribed on this Mac with Parakeet only and pasted where the text cursor is. It is never added to the library and never synced, and its audio is deleted straight after transcription. It pastes by putting the text on the clipboard (transient, this Mac only) and pressing ⌘V. The user's clipboard is put back afterwards only when macOS lets Transcribe read it ("Keep my clipboard…" in Settings → Shortcuts). Edit → Copy Last Dictation keeps the last text in memory for 10 minutes.

  The text is left on the clipboard ("Copied — press ⌘V") when:
  - Accessibility is not allowed;
  - a password field has secure input on;
  - the user switched apps before the paste;
  - the Mac went to sleep or was locked.

  Dictation is refused while a recording runs, and a recording is refused while a dictation runs ("Finish dictating first."). The shortcuts watch only their own key combinations, never store or send keystrokes, and use no network. They are off in UI-test and fake-engine runs, and while another copy of Transcribe is running. A quiet sidebar note, "Shortcuts need permission", shows while a shortcut cannot work or dictation could only copy, until the Shortcuts section of Settings has been seen once; Transcribe never asks macOS for a permission from a shortcut, only from the guided "Turn on pasting" setup there (Open Accessibility Settings, turn Transcribe on, come back; it notices the switch by itself and offers Relaunch Transcribe if macOS has not applied it).
- Updates (Mac only; the first version that has them is 0.3.0): Sparkle checks `https://w2solutions.ai/transcribe/appcast.xml`. It asks once, on the second launch that opens a window (never during a recording), "Check for updates automatically?" (once a day). Settings → Updates has that switch, Check for Updates… (also in the app menu after About), the Version row and a note on what a check sends; Check for Updates… always works, automatic or not. A check and a download send no recordings, transcripts or names: only the app's version (in the User-Agent) and what any web request carries, such as the Mac's internet address and language; no system profile. An update installs only when the user chooses Install Update, never by itself, and only from a feed and a DMG signed with the app's update key. A scheduled check never opens a window: an update it finds shows only as a quiet reminder, a small accent chip in the sidebar above the privacy line ("Update available · 0.3.1", tooltip "Transcribe 0.3.1 is ready to download. Click to see what's new."), "Update Available…" in place of Check for Updates… in the app menu, and the same title with the version on Settings → Updates. Choosing any of them opens Sparkle's update window (release notes, Install Update, Skip This Version, Remind Me Later). Check for Updates… opens it at once, as before. The reminder goes when the user installs or skips the version, or when an update session ends without Remind Me Later (an error, "You're up to date"), or when a later check finds nothing; after Remind Me Later or closing the window it stays. It is hidden during a recording and comes back when the recording stops; it is kept in memory only, so a relaunch forgets it until the next scheduled check. Install and Relaunch during a recording waits until it stops ("Update after this recording"). After an update the app prepares its models again (about a minute). Updates are off in Debug, fake-engine and UI-test runs, and in a build without a feed URL or key.

## Proposed architecture

- Native SwiftUI for both platforms, using Apple's Liquid Glass for the floating control layer only (toolbars, record dock, player bar, sheets, sidebar). Content sits on plain surfaces.
- Two thin app targets (`Transcribe` for iPhone, `TranscribeMac`) over one shared Swift package, `TranscribeKit`: capture, transcribe, separate speakers, match voices, sync. `TranscribeKit` is built on FluidAudio (see Speech stack). Its modules: `TranscribeCore`, `TranscribeKit`, `TranscribeDesign`, `TranscribeFluid`, `TranscribeFakes`, `TranscribeCloudKit`, and, for the Mac's Claude access, `TranscribeMCP` and `TranscribeMCPHost`.
- On-device stores: recordings (transcripts and speaker turns), audio files (the recording device's own WAV, or an `.m4a` copy downloaded by audio sync, both in `Audio/`), voice profiles (name + voice embeddings), and the sync bookkeeping, all in `Library.store` apart from the audio. A Mac recording of both sources also has two temporary stems in `Audio/Stems/` until it is ready. Model files are read-only resources in the app bundle (`BundledModels/`).

## Decisions

- **Voice profiles sync across devices, per microphone** (decided 2026-11-24 at Liam's request; this replaces the 2026-11-23 per-device-only decision). A profile depends on the microphone, so samples and negatives are tagged with the capture channel of their source recording. Auto-labelling needs a trusted sample from the probe's own channel. Caps apply per (profile, channel). Never add code that deletes voice data because the device or install id changed (the old device wipe is gone; `ChannelStamping` backfills channels instead).
- **Sync design** (decided 2026-11-24): `CKSyncEngine`, private database, container `iCloud.ai.w2solutions.transcribe`, one zone `Library`, over the local SwiftData store opened with `cloudKitDatabase: .none`. Never enable SwiftData's own CloudKit sync.
  - Sync state (`SyncRecordState`, `SyncAccountState`) lives in `Library.store` and is saved in the same save as the applied data.
  - Merges happen per field group, three-way against base hashes; the device saving now wins a group both sides changed, except a speaker's rejected names, which are united. Delete wins.
  - Only `.ready` recordings (and their speakers) sync. Processing state, match traces and search text stay on the device. Audio syncs separately, beside the engine and never through it: the bytes as `AudioFile` records in the private database's default zone, with a small `RecordingAudio` marker in `Library` (see "Audio sync").
  - Record fields go in `encryptedValues` (except the schema version `sv`, the transcript asset and the `AudioFile` audio asset). A record with a newer `sv` pauses sync ("Paused: update Transcribe"), except an audio marker: markers are taken out before that check, and one from a later build only makes that recording say "Update Transcribe to play this audio".
  - A full scan that would delete many records (more than 25 of a type and over 20% of them) pauses and asks before sending the deletes.
  - `TranscribeKit/Sync` has no CloudKit import; only `TranscribeCloudKit` imports CloudKit.
  - Changes arrive by iCloud push, with a check every 60 s as a safety net while the app is in front and none in the background (Liam, 2026-11-24, after pushes to the Mac proved late or missing; an earlier same-day decision of no timer at all was reversed). Do not shorten the interval or poll in the background without asking. iCloud pushes only to the app (bundle id) that created a subscription, and the two apps share the container under different bundle ids, so each app keeps its own silent `CKDatabaseSubscription` named `changes.<bundle id>` (`CloudKitTransport.ensureOwnSubscription`); the engine's single subscription only ever reached whichever app turned sync on first. A push runs `SyncCoordinator.remoteChangeNotified` through each app's `PushDelegate`. Sync also fetches when the app comes to the front (`setForeground`, at most once per 10 s) and when asked (the Sync Now toolbar button on both apps, pull down on the iPhone's Recordings list, Cmd-R on the Mac). `CloudKitTransport.fetchChanges` also catches up with its own `recordZoneChanges` token, because the engine's `fetchChanges` did not always reach the server.
  - A local change is sent 1 s after it is saved (`sendLocalChanges`); the engine's own scheduled send waited minutes on the iPhone.
  - After a send leaves work behind (a merged conflict, a retryable error, a failed send), the coordinator runs its own follow-up sync with backoff (3 s, 15 s, 60 s, then every 5 min); `CKSyncEngine` does not reliably send these again. `FakeTransport.setMaxAttemptsPerSend(1)` behaves like the engine.
  - Naming or confirming a saved voice never writes its `VoiceProfile` (`namedAt` is set once, when the profile is made), so two devices naming one person do not conflict on it.
  - Every send and fetch outcome is logged with record type, record name and CKError code as public (`/usr/bin/log show --predicate 'subsystem == "ai.w2solutions.transcribe"' --info`), never field values. The last problem shows under the Settings status line until a sync goes through. One written exception, for audio sync (decided 2026-11-26 at Liam's request): its lines (category `audio` from `AudioSync`, and `cloudkit` from `CloudKitAudioTransfer`, both in Settings → Copy sync log) may log record names (`au.<UUID>`, `af.<UUID>`), transfer sizes rounded to 0.1 MB (the `bytes` field), transfer and encode times, counts, network kind, decisions, `AudioTransferError` case names and CKError codes. Never a recording's `duration` field, the sha256 (it fingerprints the content), titles, text, names, or file paths beyond `<UUID>.<ext>`.
- **Mac app** (decided 2026-11-24): bundle id `ai.w2solutions.transcribe.mac`, App Sandbox and Hardened Runtime in every configuration, models bundled in every configuration (`TRANSCRIBE_MODELS_DIR` does not work in the sandbox). Shared views live in `Apps/Shared/Sources`; each app has `PlatformServices`; iOS-only modifiers go through `PlatformCompat`; device wording goes through `DeviceCopy`. The no-team entitlements (`TranscribeMac.local.entitlements`, for `make build-mac`, the Mac UI tests and the update end-to-end test) also have `cs.disable-library-validation`, without which a locally signed app cannot load the ad-hoc signed `Sparkle.framework`; it is never in `TranscribeMac.entitlements`, and `release-mac.sh` rejects any `cs.*` key. Archived apps have Sparkle's `Downloader.xpc` removed (an `install`-only post-build phase in `project.yml`, which re-seals the framework).
- **Mac updates (Sparkle)** (decided 2026-11-25 at Liam's request):
  - Sparkle 2.10.1, pinned in `project.yml` by revision `ab7d71291238df6e0492143b120c9b696a35f603` (a tag can move; the revision also fixes the prebuilt framework through its checksum), linked to `TranscribeMac` only. A bump changes the revision, the `2.10.1`/`2065` checks in `release-mac.sh`, the Sparkle entry in `Attributions` (version and the full licence text) and this file together. `Apps/Mac/Sources/Updates/AppUpdater.swift` is the only file that imports Sparkle; the decisions are pure and tested in `TranscribeKit/Features/Updates.swift` (`UpdateAvailability`, `UpdateReminder`, `UpdateCopy`).
  - The app downloads in-process (`network.client`, `SUEnableDownloaderService` NO) and installs through Sparkle's installer launcher (`SUEnableInstallerLauncherService`, mach-lookup `$(PRODUCT_BUNDLE_IDENTIFIER)-spks` and `-spki`). Sparkle's unsandboxed Downloader service is removed from archived apps, so the bundle holds no unsandboxed fetcher.
  - Info.plist (from `project.yml`): `SUFeedURL` = `$(TRANSCRIBE_FEED_URL)` (empty in Debug), `SUPublicEDKey` = `$(TRANSCRIBE_ED_PUBLIC_KEY)` (the public half of the update key, committed 2026-11-25; if it were empty, updates would be off and no release would build), `SURequireSignedFeed` YES with `SUSignedFeedFailureExpirationInterval` 0 (a feed whose signature fails is never accepted; Sparkle's key-rotation fallback can only be reached through a feed signed with the installed app's key), `SUVerifyUpdateBeforeExtraction` YES, `SUAllowsAutomaticUpdates` NO (the download is about 656 MB and the models are prepared again, so the user picks the moment), `SUEnableSystemProfiling` NO, `SUScheduledCheckInterval` 86400. `SUEnableAutomaticChecks` is left out on purpose, so Sparkle asks. `TRANSCRIBE_MAC_BUNDLE_ID` sets the app's bundle id alone (the end-to-end test overrides it).
  - `UpdateAvailability.resolve` turns the updater on only in a non-Debug build that is not a UI-test or fake-engine run, with an http(s) feed URL with a host and a key that is base64 of 32 bytes; the controller exists only in `#if !DEBUG` code. It starts the first time the app is active with the main window (its `.task` when `NSApp.isActive`, else its `didBecomeActiveNotification` handler), from the Settings window's `.task` or from Check for Updates…. A background launch by the Claude helper does open the main window, behind other apps, but does not start it until the user brings Transcribe to the front. It then clears any feed URL saved in the user defaults; the delegate returns only the bundled `SUFeedURL`. Never call `setFeedURL`.
  - Implementing `updaterShouldPromptForPermissionToCheck` replaces Sparkle's own second-launch rule, so the delegate repeats it (`SUHasLaunchedBefore`) as well as saying no while recording (found on 2026-11-25 in Sparkle's source while writing the end-to-end test: without it the prompt would come on the first launch).
  - No uninvited update window (decided 2026-11-25 at Liam's request; this replaces holding the alert back only during a recording): Sparkle's gentle scheduled update reminders. `standardUserDriverShouldHandleShowingScheduledUpdate` always returns NO, even "in immediate focus" (just after launch or after the Mac sat idle), so Sparkle loads the update's window but never orders it front. `UpdateReminder` (pure, in memory) holds the waiting version from `standardUserDriverWillHandleShowingUpdate` (scheduled and user-initiated), clears it on `updater(_:userDidMake:…)` Install or Skip, on `updaterDidNotFindUpdate` and on `standardUserDriverWillFinishUpdateSession` unless that session's choice was Dismiss (Remind Me Later, or the window closed). Every control calls `SPUUpdater.checkForUpdates`, which, while the scheduled driver holds the update (`showingUpdate`), calls `showUpdateInFocus` and shows the loaded window without checking again (checked in Sparkle's `SPUUpdater.m` and `SPUStandardUserDriver.m`, and by the end-to-end test: one feed request). After Remind Me Later Sparkle has ended the session, so the reminder runs a new user-initiated check. While an update waits behind the reminder, Sparkle's session stays open, so no later scheduled check runs until the user answers it or quits the app: a newer release is only seen after that (`SPUUpdater.m`: `updaterTimerDidFire` skips while `sessionInProgress`; the next check is scheduled when the driver finishes). If the user chooses Install and the download then fails or is cancelled, the reminder is gone until the next scheduled check finds the update again. The sidebar reminder, like the menu item and the Settings row, is disabled while Sparkle cannot act (`canCheckForUpdates` NO, as while a later check fetches the feed). The reminder is hidden while `recorder.isActive` (`AppUpdater` follows it for the life of the app, whichever windows are open) and comes back when the recording stops; never a window. User-initiated checks always show Sparkle's window at once, recording or not. Install and Relaunch during a recording installs when it stops. Only recording counts as busy: interrupted processing resumes after the relaunch.
  - Build number: `CFBundleVersion` = major × 10000 + minor × 100 + patch (0.3.0 is 300), the number Sparkle compares. `release-mac.sh --print-version` is its only implementation (minor and patch below 100); Debug builds stay "1". A published version is never rebuilt: a fix ships as a higher version.
  - The notarised, stapled Developer ID DMG that new users download is also the full update archive, EdDSA-signed after stapling. Its enclosure URL names the version (`/transcribe/download/Transcribe-<v>.dmg`).
  - **Delta updates** (decided 2026-11-25 at Liam's request, after the 0.3.0 → 0.3.1 update downloaded the whole 657 MB): each release also publishes one Sparkle delta from the version before, `Transcribe-<from>-<v>.delta` (BinaryDelta format 4, from the pinned Sparkle's own `BinaryDelta`), listed in `latest.json` (`deltas`: from, fromBuild, file, key, bytes, sha256, edSignature) and in the appcast item's `<sparkle:deltas>` (`sparkle:deltaFrom` = fromBuild), EdDSA-signed with the update key like the DMG. Only copies on exactly that build use it; any other copy, and any delta that fails to download, verify or apply (the installed app must match the old build byte for byte), falls back to the full DMG by itself (Sparkle's `SPUCoreBasedUpdateDriver` and `SPUInstallerDriver`; checked by `tools/update-e2e` with a tampered delta). Measured 2026-11-25: a local Release build of 0.3.0 to the published 0.3.1 is 0.9 MB (only the Mach-O files and code signatures change; the models are the same bytes); applying it rebuilt 0.3.1 byte for byte with a valid Developer ID signature, and a delta made from the published 0.3.1 DMG applies to the 0.3.1 that the in-app update installed in `/Applications`. CI fetches the published DMG through the site (`scripts/fetch-published-mac.sh`: latest.json's size and sha256, and EdDSA against the committed key), and `scripts/make-mac-delta.sh` makes the delta, applies it to the old app and compares every entry with the new one (`scripts/ci-mac-delta.sh` wraps both for the workflow). A delta is an optimisation: if making it fails, the release goes out without one (a warning in the run). The appcast's size line then names both downloads.
  - The feed is written by `scripts/sign-mac-release.sh` from a fixed template (never `generate_appcast`, which can silently write an unsigned item), one item, with plain-text notes from `release-notes/mac/<version>.txt` plus a fixed trailer (size, model preparation, where to download by hand), and signed in Sparkle's signed-feed format by the repo's own `scripts/ed25519.swift` (CryptoKit). Every signature is checked against the app's own `SUPublicEDKey`: when signing, before upload and on the read-back.
  - **The update key** (Ed25519). Liam creates it once with Sparkle's `generate_keys` from the pinned revision. The master copy is in the login keychain (service `https://sparkle-project.org`, account `ed25519`); the CI copy is the secret `SPARKLE_ED_PRIVATE_KEY` in the GitHub environment `mac-release` (branch `main` only, required reviewer Liam); one password-manager copy and one offline encrypted copy. Rules: no agent creates, imports, exports, reads or looks up the real key, ever. Never open the keychain item to a tool (never "Always Allow"): `make sign-mac` reads it with a prompt a person approves each time. Never export it under `~/Desktop` or `~/Documents` (both sync to iCloud Drive on this Mac), only to a `mktemp -d /private/tmp/sk.XXXXXX` folder, deleted at once. Never give the production key to a job that does not publish: dry runs and TEST builds use a throwaway seed. Signing never runs third-party code with the key: only `scripts/ed25519.swift`, with the key on standard input. The public half goes in `project.yml` (`TRANSCRIBE_ED_PUBLIC_KEY`; not secret).
  - What a check sends, measured by `tools/update-e2e` on 2026-11-25 (no query string; the download the same, with `Accept: */*`): `GET /transcribe/appcast.xml` with only `Host`, `Accept: application/rss+xml,*/*;q=0.1`, `Accept-Language` (the Mac's languages), `Connection`, `Accept-Encoding` and `User-Agent: Transcribe/<version> Sparkle/2.10.1`. The privacy wording (`UpdateCopy.note`, the `DeviceCopy` update line, the website) rests on this; re-measure after a Sparkle bump. With "Don't Check" nothing is requested at launch. With automatic checks off, Sparkle's update alert offers only Skip This Version and Install Update (it hides Remind Me Later then; `SUUpdateAlert.m`).
  - Key loss and rotation: see "Publishing the Mac app".
- **Claude access (MCP)** (decided 2026-11-24; pairing replaced the pasted access key on 2026-11-25): Mac only, read-only, off by default ("Let Claude read transcripts").
  - The sandboxed app serves MCP JSON-RPC (2025-11-25; 2025-06-18 accepted) at `POST /mcp` on `127.0.0.1`, JSON replies, no sessions, and pairing requests at `POST /pair`. It binds the first free of the well-known ports 41873, 44729 and 47461 (`WellKnownPorts`, shared by the app and the helper); the helper tries them in the same order, starting with the one that last answered in that helper process, and moves on to the next port after a timeout (30 s) as after any other answer that does not verify, so something that accepts and never answers on an earlier port cannot block it. When a port is taken while another copy of Transcribe runs, the status is "Another copy is sharing" and this copy stops there; only a foreign holder makes it try the next port, and with every port taken Settings says "Ports in use". Any `Origin`, or a `Host` that is not loopback, gets 403. A direct `--transport http` client is not supported.
  - **Pairing** (decided 2026-11-25 at Liam's request, replacing the access key that was pasted into Claude's config): each Claude app is allowed once in Transcribe, and no Claude config holds a key or a port. Why: any same-user unsandboxed process could already read the key from `~/.claude.json`, so an in-app Allow protects against the same things with no secret in any config and nothing to paste.
    - The helper keeps one identity per Claude app, keyed by the name from `clientInfo` in initialize ("claude-code" → "Claude Code", "claude-ai" → "Claude Desktop", else `title` or `name`, sanitised; "Claude" when there is none): a random client id and a Curve25519 key pair, plus the app's public key and the two pairing nonces once the app answered, and `verified` once a call under that pairing got a 200 or 202 that verified. It lives in `~/Library/Application Support/Transcribe MCP/` (folder 0700, one file per Claude app, 0600, written through O_EXCL temporary files); `TRANSCRIBE_MCP_STATE_DIR` moves it for tests. The app never reads that folder.
    - Pairing is commit, then reveal (`PairingCrypto`, decided 2026-11-25 after review found the first version open to a man in the middle), so nobody in between can choose the code. An unpaired tools/call sends `/pair` with the client id, name, public key and a commitment (a hash of the client id, key and a fresh nonce) to the first port that answers like Transcribe. The app answers with its public key and a fresh nonce of its own, and holds the exchange unrevealed (never shown, never "pending") for 30 s. The helper pins the app's key and both nonces, then sends its nonce to the same port; the app checks it against the commitment (a mismatch gets 400 and ends the exchange) and only then shows the request. The pairing transcript (client id, both keys, both nonces) gives both the 6-digit code and the HMAC key (X25519, then HKDF-SHA256 bound to the transcript). The helper returns an error result asking the user to check the code and click Allow in Transcribe; it never waits.
    - The same commitment again gets the same app nonce; a new commitment from the same client id and key while unrevealed restarts with a fresh nonce; a client id that is already pending, allowed, or tied to another key gets 409 (a nonce is never reused with another commitment); a client held as denied gets "denied".
    - The app shows the request as a prompt on the main window ("Allow “Claude Code” to read your transcripts?") and inline in Settings → Claude, each with the code, Allow and Don't Allow, and bounces the Dock icon once without taking focus. In the prompt, Return and Esc both mean Don't Allow, and Allow needs a click (decided 2026-11-25 after review): any local process can make a request appear, and a Return pressed while typing must never grant access. It is a sheet with its own buttons (`ClaudePairingAlert`), because a SwiftUI alert gives Return to Allow, or loses Esc when Don't Allow takes Return. Allow saves the app with its pairing; Don't Allow refuses it for 5 minutes, then forgets it, so a later request asks again. At most 3 exchanges wait at once, unrevealed and pending together, and the app hands out at most 5 nonces in 10 minutes, restarts included; more get 429 (not queued, never displacing one already there). The nonce limit also bounds how often anyone can try for a code that matches Claude's: one chance in a million per try. Requests expire after 10 minutes and are dropped, with unrevealed exchanges, when the switch goes off. `/pair` is answered only while the switch is on, with only the status, the app's public key and its nonce.
    - Signed calls name the client and its pairing in `Authorization` (`TranscribeHMAC client=…, pub=…, pairing=<transcript>, ts=…, nonce=…, mac=…`; ±60 s, the port and the client id, key and transcript in the MAC, and not signed before the listener began serving). Only the exact client id, key and transcript the user allowed are allowed. An allowed client gets the MCP reply, each nonce once; a pending, denied or unknown one gets a signed 403 saying which, never data, and its nonce is not kept (anyone can sign as a client of their own making, so only allowed clients may fill the replay guard). A client the app has no record of whose MAC fails gets an unsigned 401 marked unknown. Every reply after the MAC check is signed with that client's key; the helper trusts only replies that verify, tries the next port when one does not, and reports that something other than Transcribe answered when none does.
    - When the app says, signed with the pairing's key, that it does not know the client (removed in Settings, the request expired, or the denial hold ended), the helper deletes that identity and asks again with a new one. An unsigned 401 marked unknown never makes it drop or replace a verified pairing (one that has had a verified 200 or 202), because anything on a port can send one: the helper keeps it and tells the user that something answered for Transcribe that it could not verify, to check that Transcribe is open and sharing, and, if Transcribe no longer lists this Claude app under Allowed apps, to delete `~/Library/Application Support/Transcribe MCP` and ask again. That is the only way back for a verified pairing after the app lost its key (its `ClaudeAccess.json` was deleted); a Debug session with `TRANSCRIBE_MCP=on` makes a new key each launch, which is why `mcp-smoke.sh` deletes its helper state in step 5. A pairing that has never been verified (decided 2026-11-25 after review) is replaced by a new request, as for a first call, when nothing verifies and every port that answers says, unsigned, that it does not know the client; a plain 401, an HTTP error, a dropped connection, a timeout or a 503 anywhere keeps it, since that may be the paired app itself. No pairing the user allowed and used is ever lost this way.
    - Accepted residual risks, to revisit before adding any write tool: (1) the helper's private keys are readable by any same-user unsandboxed process, which could then act as that allowed Claude app while the switch is on (as with the old key in `~/.claude.json`; Settings says so). (2) The helper cannot tell Transcribe from something else that speaks the pairing protocol: it pairs with whatever answers a pairing request first, in port order. So a process (of any user, or a sandboxed app with `network.server`) holding an earlier well-known port can capture a new helper's pairing even while the user's Transcribe runs and answers on a later port; the user then sees a code in Claude and no request in their own Transcribe. The plain case needs no attacker: two macOS users on one Mac each running Transcribe, where the first to start holds 41873, so the other user's Claude apps pair with it. While that holder answers signed, the helper keeps asking it (and sees its tool arguments, and could be given made-up content); once nothing answers that knows the pairing, a never-verified pairing is replaced and the user's own Transcribe is asked (see above), but a pairing that holder ever answered with a verified 200 stays until the user deletes the helper's state. It cannot obtain transcripts: its own request to Transcribe shows a code that matches Claude's only by a one-in-a-million chance per try, at most 5 tries per 10 minutes, and the user sees no request at all unless it reveals. A verified pairing with the user's Transcribe is never moved to a new pairing by such a process (see the unsigned 401 above). The fix is an open decision (checking who owns the listening port).

- **Voice sample groups** (decided 2026-11-26 at Liam's request): on a person's voice screen (iPhone and Mac) their samples are grouped by how they sound, so the same person close to the microphone, far away and through a loudspeaker shows as separate groups.
  - Groups are derived, never stored and never synced: `SampleGrouping` (pure, in `TranscribeCore`) runs average-linkage clustering per capture channel each time the screen draws, joining while the average cosine is at least `SampleGroupPolicy.joinCosine` = 0.65 (an untested starting value: tune it on real close, far and loudspeaker samples). Samples of another voice model, or still syncing, show under "Not grouped". Since 2026-11-26 the same groups also decide auto-labelling and which samples curation keeps (see "Matching and curation by sound group"); labels never change matching.
  - The result must not depend on input order or on the device, so every comparison uses scores rounded to four places with id tie-breaks, and sums run in a fixed order. `VoiceVector.cosine` must stay a fixed-order scalar loop (no vDSP or simd).
  - A label is optional ("Group 2" until the user names it, at most 40 characters). It is a `VoiceGroupLabel` row holding the packed vectors of the group's members when it was saved (its anchor), not sample ids or a group number. `GroupLabelResolver` puts each label on the group that still holds its anchor samples (bit-equal vectors), else on one whose members average 0.65 or more to them, one label per group. So a label follows its group as samples are added or evicted; when two labelled groups merge, one label shows and the other is kept and returns if they separate. Read anchors only with `VoiceVector.unpacked` (`init(packed:)` normalises again and changes bits).
  - Only Save and Remove Label write a label, and only the one the group shows (`LibraryStore.setGroupLabel`, `removeGroupLabel`; a group that changed since the screen drew it throws `StoreError.groupChanged`). Merging two people copies the labels across; re-enrolling a person on a new voice model deletes their labels with their old samples.
  - Labels sync (decided 2026-11-26 at Liam's request, replacing the same day's "labels stay on the device"): record type `VoiceGroupLabel` (record names `vg.<UUID>`), one field group (`profileID`, `text`, `createdAt`, `editedAt`, `embeddingModelKey`, `captureChannel`, `anchor`, `anchorDimension`), all in `encryptedValues` like every other field. The anchor and the samples' vectors travel as their stored bytes and are stored as received, never decoded and packed again, so a label matches its group bit for bit on every device. No store schema change and no `sv` change: a build without the type (0.3.2 and earlier) skips records whose name prefix it does not know. A label that arrives before its voice or its samples is kept and shows once they arrive (its group is worked out when drawn). A sole group heard through Mac audio still shows its header.

- **Matching and curation by sound group** (decided 2026-11-26 at Liam's request: decisions D3 and D4; `MatchPolicy.groupScoring`, on by default).
  - Groups are `SampleGrouping`'s, over the profile's samples on exactly the probe's channel with the probe's dimension, joined at `MatchPolicy.groupJoinCosine` (= `SampleGroupPolicy.joinCosine`, 0.65, the one source), made inside the matcher (`GroupScoring`) and cached per (profile, channel) within one decision. A candidate without `sampleIDs` is scored without groups.
  - Two scores. W, the whole score, is today's pair score (the best trusted sample on the channel first, then the best of the rest, other channels included); it decides asking (W ≥ 0.40), the greedy order, blocking (`negativeMax ≥ W`) and conflicting negatives. A, the auto score, is the best group score over groups on the probe's channel that have a trusted member: a group of two or more scores its own pair score (both places from inside the group); a one-sample group scores that sample's own cosine (rule S2: it no longer borrows second place from other environments, which fixed "a 0.60 desk voice drops to 0.425 once a far sample joins"). So a channel's samples from other sound groups, and samples from other channels, never fill a place in A. With one trusted sample on the probe's channel, a new voice is auto-labelled on that sample alone (0.55 or more), however well other channels match.
  - Auto-label when not downgraded, not ask-only (no trusted sample on the channel, or a `mixed` speaker), A ≥ 0.55, A − R ≥ 0.10 and `negativeMax < A`. The runner-up R is symmetric: max(0, the cohort, and for every other profile the larger of its W and its best group score G, any group, trusted or not). New trace reasons: `groupScore` (the matcher without groups would have auto-labelled: W against the runner-up it used, the cohort and other profiles' W; A or the symmetric runner-up stops it), `negativeNear`, `mixedSource`; `belowAuto` and `margin` are judged on A. The auto decision carries A and A − R.
  - Automatic samples: the existing gates (margin 0.20, coherence 0.5, 10 s), with the trusted score measured in the winning group g* (its top two trusted when it has two, else the channel's top two trusted), at least 0.70, and the voice must join g*'s centroid (q(cosine) ≥ q(0.65)) (`SampleCurator.shouldAddAutomaticSample(decision:evidence:...)`).
  - Curation per (profile, channel): a soft cap of 12 and a ceiling of 20. Over 12, protected samples are the new one, the earliest `.named` one, and one representative of every group (taken before the insert) that has a non-automatic member (its first trusted member, else its first short one); groups of automatic samples only are not protected. Unprotected samples go first: automatic, then short, then trusted; then from the largest group (recounted as samples go); then the most redundant in its group; then the oldest. Over 20, protected samples go too (never the new one or the earliest named): by class, then the one closest to another group, then the oldest. Merging two people goes through `SampleCurator.insertMany` once per (channel, dimension), with both sides' groups and representatives protected; a copied sample keeps its date and stored bytes and gets a new id.
  - `groupScoring = false` reproduces the earlier matcher and curator exactly (checked against the frozen `GrouplessMatcher` in the tests, and every `GoldenMatcher` scenario).
  - Match trace v3 (`MatchTrace.groupsVersion`; v1 and v2 still decode): per profile `autoScore`, `groupCount`, `groupSize`, `groupTrustedCount`, `groupBestScore`, `autoTrustedScore`, `autoCentroidCosine` and the S1 value `singletonBorrowShadow`; per speaker `speakerSource` and `systemShare` (Mac audio recordings only).
- **Mac system audio** (decided 2026-11-26 at Liam's request; Mac only).
  - Mac audio stays on the recording's channel (`mac`; decision D2, after a Slack call through the speakers auto-labelled Tom correctly against his `mac` samples). Never add a `system` capture channel or channel families; calls are kept apart from the room by sound groups.
  - Core Audio process taps, no ScreenCaptureKit (`TranscribeFluid/SystemAudioCapture.swift`, pure rules in `TapRing.swift` and `TapClock.swift`, all `#if os(macOS)`): a private, unmuted mono global tap that excludes Transcribe by bundle id (`bundleIDs`) and by its audio object (when the process has none yet, as in a Mac-audio-only recording, it watches `kAudioHardwarePropertyProcessObjectList` and looks again after 0.1 to 5 s, and sets the tap's description again with the object as soon as it appears; in-app playback stays enabled during a recording, so until then the bundle id is the only exclusion), on a private aggregate device (`tapautostart` off, the tap with drift compensation on). Its clock (`MacAudioClock`, `TapClockPlan`; 2026-11-27): first `tap`, the tap alone with no sub-device list and no main sub-device (as Chromium's `catap_audio_input_stream.mm` and Apple's corespeechd do), its nominal rate set to 48 kHz and read back (the HAL applies a rate asynchronously, so after a set that succeeds the rate is read again every 10 ms until it is 48 kHz, at most 0.5 s, `TapRateSettle`; a set that fails is ignored and the rate read is used); then `builtIn`, the built-in output device (transport type built-in, with output streams) as main sub-device; then `output`, the default output as main sub-device (the only composition before 2026-11-27, kept as the last resort). A mode fails when it cannot be created or started, reads back no rate (`tap`, `builtIn`) or, after a successful set, not 48 kHz within 0.5 s (`tap`: "rate read back X, set 48000"), has other input buffers than the tap's (for `tap`, exactly the tap's) or than `TapBufferLayout.place` accounts for, or delivers no IO cycle within 2 s of `AudioDeviceStart`; that logs "Mac audio: <mode> clock failed (<reason>); trying <next>", tears it down and tries the next. The receiver reports its first IO cycle (`onFirstIO`), which confirms the mode; a watchdog checks at 2 s, each with its own token, so one armed before a pause, a confirmation or a later arm does nothing (a resume in place arms a fresh one). The mode that delivered its first IO is kept for the recording (rebuilds start from it, then the others in order); only with `output` does a new default output since start again from `tap`. A mode that started and delivered no IO is left out of later attempts for the recording unless every mode has (`TapClockPlan.noIO`), and a rebuild with no list of its own while a mode still waits for its first IO restarts that mode and the ones after it, never the ones the watchdog already left. When all fail, the kept mode is forgotten and the build is a failed build (lost, retried as below). DEBUG builds only: the user default `macAudioClock` (`tap`, `builtIn`, `output`) picks the first mode, the others still following (`open Transcribe.app --args -macAudioClock output`; read only in `#if DEBUG`). Why: with Bose QC headphones in call mode (HFP, 16 kHz) as the aggregate's clock, coreaudiod logged "HALS_IORawClock::Update: Re-anchoring IO timeline" about 100 times a second for 0 to 12.5 s from within about 20 ms of our IO start, about 30% of IO cycles came late or not at all, and Mac audio was choppy (2026-11-27). The `tap` and `builtIn` clocks are NOT yet tested on hardware (unknown: whether a tap-only aggregate runs IO during silence, its rate in call mode, and whether the storm comes from the microphone's own aggregate instead); only `output` has run on a real Mac. The IO block delivers the tap at the aggregate's rate (`TapStreamFormat.ioFormat`: the aggregate's nominal rate, else the tap stream's virtual format, else the tap's): with `output`, Bluetooth headphones in call mode run at 16 kHz while `kAudioTapPropertyFormat` and the tap stream's virtual format both still say 48 kHz (found 2026-11-27: read as 48 kHz, two thirds of every chunk became zeros and the audio sounded robotic); the HAL resamples the tap to the aggregate's rate. The receiver also measures the rate (`TapRateCheck`: the device's sample time against host time, else the frames that arrive, over 1.5 s windows of steady IO; two windows in a row more than 8% off the rate in use and within 3% of the same standard rate are a verdict). A verdict rebuilds the aggregate ("Mac audio arrives at X Hz, not Y Hz; rebuilding"), at most twice a recording; only after that does the receiver convert from the measured rate, when the capture tells it (`TapRateResponse`); the receiver never swaps its converter on its own. Not frames alone: while the Bose headphones switched into call mode the HAL skipped about 30% of IO cycles, and counting frames read that as 11,025 Hz and stretched the audio (2026-11-27). Skipped cycles are lost audio: within one continuous run (host gaps of 0.5 s at most, as far as the aligner's hard correction; sample time increasing) the receiver delivers zeros for the frames the sample time skipped (`TapGapFill`: when the host time agrees within 25%; the skipped frames as zeros at the input rate, converted like the audio so a resampler's held tail of the previous buffer comes out before them, stamped at the end of the previous buffer, before the next one), so Mac audio stays contiguous and correctly timed even without the aligner; a pause is never filled. At stop it logs "the output skipped N frames of IO (…; M filled in place)" and "dropped N tap buffers". When the clock device has inputs of its own (AirPods or a USB headset as the default output, `output` only in practice), the tap's buffers are picked by position (`TapBufferLayout.place`: by stream object id when the aggregate reports the device's own streams; otherwise the order, inputs before or after the tap, whose buffers match the device's own input configuration; a stream whose terminal type is a microphone is never the tap; when a mono microphone and a mono tap still fit either way, inputs first, logged as not certain), and the other input streams are turned off for the IOProc (`kAudioDevicePropertyIOProcStreamUsage`; never needed with `tap`, whose list is the tap's alone). The IOProc block only copies the tap's buffers and host and sample times into a preallocated ring (`TapRing`, which also counts IO cycles) and signals; another queue converts to 16 kHz mono and delivers with host-time stamps. The ring drops any IO cycle whose buffer count is not the one the aggregate was built with, and the first such cycle rebuilds it (at most 3 per recording; after that Mac audio is reported lost, and only a later output or device change tries again). The layout is read once more after 50 ms while it is empty or does not yet list the clock device's own inputs. Changes (`TapRebuildPolicy`, pure, tested; 2026-11-27, after an output switch rebuilt the aggregate every 0.29 s for over 20 s, 87 times, because the default-output listeners compared nothing and each create or destroy of our aggregate made coreaudiod pick the defaults again): every notice is compared with what was built. A new default output (or default system output) never rebuilds with `tap` or `builtIn` (the global tap follows every app's output); it only notes the route in the capture detail (`routeChanged`, and the output's name, model and route now) when its UID really changed. With `output`, or while lost, it rebuilds only when its UID differs from the one the last build was for. A changed tap format, sub-device or tap list, input streams (when they differ from what it was built with) or IO format, or an aggregate or clock device (built-in or output) that is no longer alive, rebuilds. Rebuilds keep the tap. Rebuilds that notices ask for are spaced by `TapRebuildLimiter`: the first after 250 ms, one sooner than a second after the last rebuild waits until the second has passed, and notices while one is pending join it. Retries, resumes and clock fallbacks rebuild at once and count as the last rebuild; a pending notice rebuild whose notices all came before such a rebuild is dropped (that rebuild used the current state), and one with a later notice waits until a second after it. So notice rebuilds are at least a second after any rebuild; only the direct ones can come sooner. Each notice is logged at debug or info, "Mac audio notice: <what> → ignored|rebuild|rebuild on resume" (public, no names). During a pause a change that rebuilds (or a rebuild it had already scheduled) is remembered and `resume()` rebuilds instead of restarting the old aggregate (`TapResumePlan`: same tap format, aggregate alive, and the same output UID only with `output`). A failed build is "lost" (`.systemAudioLost`, once) and is tried again on every later change that differs from the last attempt and after 1, 2, 5, 10 and 30 s (`TapRetryPolicy`) unless paused. A build that starts is not yet a success (review 2026-11-27): only its first IO cycle clears lost, resets the failure count and sends `.systemAudioRestored`, which in Both mode mixes Mac audio in again (`StreamAligner.markRestored`) and clears the note. So clocks that all start and never deliver IO count as one more failed build per chain and back off along those delays, instead of reporting lost and restored every few seconds; after a resume while lost, the next loss is reported again (a Mac-audio-only recording resumed itself and must pause again). In a Both recording a Mac audio that cannot start counts as lost from the start and keeps trying the same way (`keepsTryingAfterFailedStart`). These rules are `TapLifecycle` (pure, tested). The backlog is derived on every run, never stored, so recordings made before this build or while the switch was off upload by themselves. On the iPhone work starts only while the app is in front; a transfer in flight when it leaves holds `BackgroundTaskBridge.beginShortTask` and is cancelled when that expires, to run again next time in front. The Mac always counts as in front (`AudioSync.setForeground(false)` does nothing there). Work starts only from existing triggers: the end of every sync round (push, coming to the front, the 60 s check, Sync Now, local changes), `.fetchFinished`, send results, the switch, opening a transcript, and a network or power change while in front. Transfers are not resumable (a retry starts again, cheap at AAC sizes); retries per recording, in memory: 1 min, 10 min, 1 h, then every 6 h. A crash after the blob upload is repaired by the probe (adopted, never sent twice); a crash after a download's move by `LibraryStore.adoptStrayDownloads` at launch. No long-lived operations in this version.
  - Epoch and account: `AudioSync.epoch` goes up on `stop()`, `reset()` and the start of a removal. A job captures it and after every `await` touches the store only while it is unchanged and `SyncCoordinator.audioMayRun` holds. Once `transfer.upload` has returned, the job writes its marker even if the switch went off or the background task ended; only an epoch change abandons it (the blob stays and the next probe adopts it). Before every job, `SyncCoordinator.currentAccountMatches()` (the transport's account hash equals the stored one) must be true, else the loop ends until the coordinator handles the account change.
  - iCloud full on an audio upload pauses audio uploads only, for 24 h ("Audio uploads paused: iCloud storage is full."; cleared by Sync Now, a relaunch or a successful upload); downloads, deletes and every `Library` record carry on. A `quotaExceeded` on a `Library` save (a marker included) is the usual whole-sync pause. CloudKit refuses only the request that crosses the quota, so audio can take the last free space and the next transcript save then pauses all sync: the copy never promises "Transcripts still sync", and that pause says how much iCloud storage synced audio uses (`SyncCopy.quotaAudioLine`, from `AudioSync.bytesInICloud`).
  - State, with no store schema change and no `sv` bump: one `SyncRecordState` row per known marker, with the foreign `typeRaw` `"AudioState"`, record name `au.<UUID>`, `pendingRaw` 0, no system fields, no unresolved links, and its facts (`AudioRowFacts`: version `v`, descriptor, marker `.sending`/`.onServer`/`.deleting`, marker version, `recordingSeen`, `firstSeenAt`) as JSON under the key `~audio` in `baseHashesJSON`. A `typeRaw` that is no `SyncRecordType` gives a nil `recordID`, so every generic path (full scan, breaker, `pendingCount`, `finishFetch`, a 0.3.3 build after a downgrade) skips the row, and `clearSyncState()` deletes it with everything else, so an environment change, an account merge, a zone removal and Remove iCloud Copy reset audio state with no extra code. Progress, failures, backoff, the quota block, probe answers and per-launch counters live in memory. Scratch folders: `AudioOutgoing/<id>.m4a` (encodes waiting to upload, written as `.tmp` and renamed) and `AudioIncoming/<id>.<ext>.part` (downloads in progress), both left out of backups (iOS protection `.completeUntilFirstUserAuthentication`) and cleaned at launch (`AudioSync.prepareOnLaunch`). Downloaded copies in `Audio/` stay in device backups: they are user data, and after Remove iCloud Copy a backup may hold the only other copy. `AppSettings.syncAudioLastSweep` (`sync.audio.lastSweep`) and `syncAudioIntroShown` (`sync.audio.introShown`) are bookkeeping, not user settings.
  - The core never sees `.audio` records. `SyncCoordinator` partitions markers out of `.fetched`, `.sent` and the provider before the `sv` check and before `RemoteApplier`, and hands them to `AudioSync`. `SyncRecordType.audio` exists only so the codec, transports and fakes carry markers (`isLibraryEntity` false; `SyncRecordType.libraryCases` is for tests and new loops, while `ChangeDetector`'s full scan still iterates `allCases` and is safe only because the projector returns nothing for `.audio` and no state row has its `typeRaw`: audio rows use `"AudioState"`); `RecordProjector` returns nothing for it and `remoteInsert` treats it as unreachable. `pendingCount()` adds the markers waiting to go, so `sendAll` sends them and "Up to date" waits for them. `ChangeDetector`, `RemoteApplier`, `MergePolicy`, `InvariantRepair` and `Schema.swift` did not change for audio.
  - Delete wins, and audio follows the recording's deletion, never mere absence. A marker row is gone when the device has seen its recording (`recordingSeen`), the `Recording` row no longer exists and no `rec.<id>` state row exists: a local delete keeps its row until the server acknowledges it, an applied remote delete removes it at once, and a parked delete or a mass-delete hold keeps it. Then the blob is deleted, then the marker (bytes before marker, so a failure leaves at most a marker pointing at nothing, which heals itself). Every new build that sees a delete does it, so deletes made on 0.3.3 are cleaned up too. The mass-delete breaker needed no change: held deletes keep their audio, Restore finds it still in iCloud, Confirm deletes it. A marker that arrives before its recording waits (`recordingSeen` false) and is never read as a delete; a row whose recording never appears is kept.
  - A missing blob behind a marker (a download's `notFound`) is confirmed by a second probe before anything changes; only a confirmed absence makes the downloading device delete the marker, and the device holding the audio then sees no marker and uploads again. Brakes: at most 25 such marker deletes per launch (then it stops and notes the sync problem "Some audio in iCloud couldn't be found."), and at most 3 uploads of one recording per launch. A metadata probe's `.unreadable` (after `encryptedDataReset`) is confirmed with a full-key fetch (`probeFully`) before a holder deletes and uploads again.
  - Remove iCloud Copy… / Also Remove from iCloud: the dialog first says how many ready recordings here have a known marker and no local file (`AudioSync.cloudOnlyCount`) and offers Download Audio First (those downloads forced on any network, with the room checks; the dialog closes and sync stays on). The button shows only while it can download (`AudioSync.downloadAvailability == .available`: Sync audio on here, sync running and not paused). With Sync audio off here the dialog gives no count (markers a 0.3.3 build dropped are learnt only by the launch probe, which needs Sync audio) and says audio from the other devices may be only in iCloud and to turn on Sync audio to download it first; while sync is paused (an iCloud that is full, say) it adds "It can download once sync resumes.". The removal, inside `ownsSignedInZone()` and before `deleteZone()`: `beginRemoval()`, `await stopAndWait()` (the loop and the transfer in flight have ended), then delete every blob it can name (audio rows, local recordings, `rec.` rows and the query listing, best effort), 200 per request; after `deleteZone()` it lists once more and deletes what it finds (best effort), for another device's upload whose marker landed between the listing and the zone delete. A failed delete throws: the zone, markers and switch stay, this device uploads no audio until a removal succeeds or the app relaunches (`removalFailed`), and since deletes are not atomic the message says some audio may already have been removed. Local audio files, downloaded copies included, are never touched. Another device whose marker save meets the missing zone (or that gets `.zoneDeleted(.deleted/.purged)`) first deletes, best effort, the blobs it uploaded whose markers the server never acknowledged and the blob of the upload in flight, which may be stored with no row yet (`uploadsToAbandon()` captured before `clearSyncState()`, then `abandonUploadsAfterStop`, which waits for that upload to end before deleting).
  - Orphan sweep, by server truth: at most weekly, in front, only after a fetch that succeeded since the last `clearSyncState()` (`SyncCoordinator.fetchSucceededSinceReset`), and never in the first 7 days after audio sync first ran on the device (`syncAudioLastSweep` is set to that moment). It lists every `AudioFile` (needs the QUERYABLE `recordName` index; without it it logs and does nothing), and for blobs created over 24 h ago that are not a local recording's and not being uploaded here asks the server whether `rec.<id>` exists in `Library` (`recordingRecordsExist`, metadata only). Only blobs whose `rec.` the server reports missing are orphans: the blob is deleted, then its marker. A missing `Library` zone aborts the sweep.
  - Conflicts are trivial because audio is immutable per recording id: a second device's upload meets `serverRecordChanged` and adopts the server's descriptor; a second marker save takes the server's marker. A download is verified (size and sha256, off the main actor) against the descriptor returned with the blob only; when that differs from the row's, the row takes the blob's (logged), so a marker that disagrees with its blob never wedges a recording.
  - Sync audio off on a device: no uploads, no downloads, the transfer in flight cancelled (unless it is a delete, the sweep, or an upload already writing its marker); local files, downloaded copies and iCloud audio stay; markers are still received, so it can say "Audio is in iCloud", and deletes caused by deleting recordings still go through. Turning it on resumes the derived backlog.
  - Older builds (0.3.3 and earlier, both apps) never see `af.` records (default zone), download and drop `au.` markers (unknown prefix, as 0.3.2 dropped `vg.`) and are never paused (marker `sv` 1, `SyncSchema.version` unchanged), so no minimum version is needed. The markers a 0.3.3 build dropped are not fetched again after it updates (both change tokens moved past them), so a launch probe recovers them: once per launch and after every `reset()`, while audio may run, `AudioSync` probes (metadata only, at most 200 ids per request) every ready recording with no local file and no audio row. `.present` makes an `.onServer` row; `.absent` is remembered for the launch; `.unreadable` makes nothing. A transient error (`.retryable`: an intermittent network, rate limiting, a busy server) is no answer: those recordings stay "checking" and are asked again after the per-recording backoff (or CloudKit's `retryAfter`, if longer; Sync Now clears it), while other work goes on; only after `AudioPolicy.launchProbeTries` (3) such failures in a row are they read as absent for the launch. Other errors read the chunk as absent, as before; no network or no account ends the loop. Until this launch's probe has answered for a recording, its transcript says "Checking iCloud for the audio…", never "isn't in iCloud yet". A refetch of the zone was rejected for this: it would re-run the core applier over the whole zone and needs transport changes.
  - One-time notice for existing sync users: `introPending` is true while sync and Sync audio are on, `syncAudioIntroShown` is false, sync is running and the first derive of the launch finds uploads or downloads left. The Library card ("Audio now syncs with iCloud", with the backlog's count and size) has Turn Off (`syncAudio` off, flag set) and OK (flag set). `SyncCoordinator.enable()` (turning sync on with the switch, merges and resets) sets the flag, so new sync users never see it; `start()` at launch does not. Turning sync or Sync audio off in Settings instead takes the card away for that launch without marking it seen (it shows only while sync runs).
  - Views notice a landed file through the `audioFileName` save (`@Query` screens) and `AudioSync.revision` (every screen that checks `hasLocalAudio` reads it); `TranscriptView` loads the player when `hasAudio` turns true. A download only writes a file and `audioFileName`: it never calls `enqueue` or `retry` and never touches the stage or attempt count, so audio from iCloud never starts processing.
  - Claude access and matching are untouched: no MCP code reads audio, `audioFileName` or audio state (`audioNeverReachesClaude`), and `VoiceMatcher`, `SampleCurator`, `MatchPolicy`, `Rematcher` and the matching log did not change.
  - Hashing and encoding never run on the main actor: `FileDigest.sha256(of:)` and the encoder are `async` and run off it (`@concurrent`); `AudioSync` (main actor) only awaits them.
  - Logging: categories `audio` and `cloudkit` under the written exception in "Sync design" (rounded sizes and times yes; a recording's duration and the sha256 never). `CKOperationGroup` names are fixed words: `audio-upload`, `audio-download`, `audio-delete`, `audio-list`, `audio-probe`, `audio-check`.
  - Debug builds with the real engine default Sync audio to off. A team-signed Debug Mac build shares its container and UserDefaults with `/Applications/Transcribe.app` and uses the Development database, so a default-on switch would upload the real library's audio there. `AppSettings(defaults:syncAudioDefault:)` takes the default, and `Composition` passes false under `#if DEBUG` unless the run uses the fake engine or UI tests. The key is written only when the user flips the switch, so Release builds read the absent key as on.
  - Never: put an asset on a record the engine syncs, or in a custom zone; fetch `af.` records without `desiredKeys`, except the download and the `probeFully` confirmation; write a marker before its blob is in iCloud, or delete a marker before its blob (bytes before marker, both ways); let the core see a `.audio` record (audio rows use `typeRaw` `"AudioState"`; route every `.audio` id to `AudioSync` before any generic state-row lookup or insert, because the row shares the marker's record name and `SyncRecordState.recordName` is unique); move the marker partition after the `sv` check; **delete a blob because this device lacks a row or a recording: the sweep deletes only when the server has no `rec.<id>`**; act on a `notFound` or an `.unreadable` before it is confirmed; forget an audio row for its age, or delete a row whose facts carry a higher `v` than the build knows (a newer build sharing the Mac's container wrote it: it is kept untouched, shows "Update Transcribe to play this audio" and no job acts on it; only an unparseable or malformed row is deleted); add an audio timer or background polling, or change the 60 s interval for audio.

## Speech stack (decided 2026-11-23)

Liam confirmed FluidAudio after three independent research passes all picked it. On a real iPhone 15 all three models load and the setup is measured (2026-11-25); speed, memory and accuracy on real recordings there are not measured yet.

- **One Swift package: FluidAudio** (`FluidInference/FluidAudio`, Apache-2.0, Core ML). It covers all three jobs:
  - Transcription: Parakeet TDT v3 via `AsrManager`.
  - Speaker separation after recording: Nemotron 3 fast128 is the primary separator, on the CPU; `OfflineDiarizerManager` (pyannote Community-1 pipeline) is the fallback and also the embedder.
  - Voice embeddings: WeSpeaker ResNet34, 256 numbers per voice, from `DiarizationResult.speakerDatabase` or `embedSpans(...)`.
- **We write the voice matcher ourselves.** FluidAudio's `SpeakerManager` only works with its streaming diariser, not the offline one. Our matcher stores embeddings per named person and compares new speakers by cosine similarity after normalising the vectors.
- **Runner-up: sherpa-onnx** (Apache-2.0, ONNX Runtime). Also one package, with a ready-made enrol/search manager, but CPU by default and no published accuracy figures.
- **Ruled out:** Apple's Speech frameworks (no speaker separation), Argmax open tier (no Parakeet; Parakeet is paid with a 1,000-device minimum), Picovoice (needs online key validation), FluidAudio's CAM++ embedder (trained on Mandarin speakers).

Rules that follow from the research:

- Run transcription and speaker separation one after the other, never at once, and lower the diariser batch size (about 8) on iPhone to limit memory.
- **Nemotron 3 runs on the CPU only** (decided by Liam on 2026-11-25): `NemotronSeparation.computeUnits = .cpuOnly`, pinned by `presetIsTheFullPrecisionFast128Bundle`. Parakeet and community-1 stay on the Neural Engine (`.cpuAndNeuralEngine`). Nothing uses the GPU (iOS forbids it in the background). Why: on the iPhone 15 (A16, iOS 27 build 24B156) Nemotron's Neural Engine compile took 55–58 s of a 76–83 s cold setup, after every install; on the CPU its cold load is 0.89 s and the cold setup 20.7 s (Parakeet 18.8 s, diarizer 0.98 s, warm-up 0.06 s). A relaunch of the same build sets up in 0.4–1.1 s. On an M4 Max (indicative only): cold load 0.5–0.65 s on the CPU against about 30 s on the Neural Engine, 46 ms against 24 ms per 10.24 s chunk, outputs within 0.0002 on random input. Smaller or quantised exports did not compile faster, compiles run one at a time, and a compile at `.background` QoS was 7x slower. Setup still loads Nemotron once (about 1 s) to check it loads; a failure there makes separation use community-1 for the process. Not yet measured: CPU separation time and battery cost on a real recording on the iPhone, and speaker-label parity with the Neural Engine on real speech. Do not move it back to the Neural Engine without asking Liam.
- Store the embedding model name and version with every voice profile. A model change means re-enrolling everyone. Models change only with an app update; a change to the speaker-diarization rows in `scripts/models.lock` requires bumping `EmbeddingModelID.revision`.
- Keep several embeddings per person, not one average: 12 per capture channel, up to a ceiling of 20 so that no sound group with a user-confirmed sample loses its last member (see "Matching and curation by sound group"). A sample's channel is its source recording's, never the device where the user clicked, and a new sample never evicts one from another channel. Store one whenever the user names, confirms or corrects a saved person (5 s of clean speech or more; under 10 s it is a "short" sample). Store one automatically only when the person's user-confirmed samples alone match at 0.70 or more, measured in the winning sound group on the probe's channel, the voice joins that group, and it is 0.20 clear of the runner-up, from 10 s of coherent speech (span coherence ≥ 0.5).
- The whole score W = mean of the two best sample cosines. The best one must be a user-confirmed sample of 10 s or more **from the probe's channel**. Samples from other channels, automatic samples and short samples can only fill second place. Without such a sample the voice can only be asked about, never auto-labelled. W decides asking; auto-labelling is decided on the group score A (a group's own pair score, or a one-sample group's own cosine, on the probe's channel only), with a symmetric runner-up and no negative at A or above.
- When the user says a voice is not P (No on a suggestion, naming a suggested voice as someone else, or correcting a wrong label), store that voice as a negative for P (5 s or more, up to 8 per person per channel). Negatives from every channel count. P is not offered for a voice at least as close to a negative as to P itself. A blocked P still counts as runner-up. Naming that voice as P later deletes the conflicting negatives. With Remember off, nothing is stored.
- The auto-label margin also counts unnamed voices that do not score as P (the cohort), so a recurring never-named person cannot be auto-labelled as P. The window is the last 100 recordings (and at most 300 voices) per capture channel, so recordings synced from another device never push this device's voices out; every channel's voices count.
- A speaker's vector is the weighted mean of its span embeddings after dropping spans under 0.35 cosine to the weighted medoid, as long as at least half the speech remains.
- No per-person thresholds. Thresholds change only after reading real numbers from Settings → Copy voice matching log (version 3: samples and trusted samples per channel, group sizes and trusted groups per channel, Mac audio samples per channel, each recording's channel, input route, sources and output route, each speaker's source, and the policy including group scoring, the join value, the ceiling and the attribution constants; never names, group ids, labels or anchors).
- Never name a single short turn on its own. Name the whole speaker for the recording; leave overlapping speech out of embeddings.
- Three outcomes for a match: auto-label, ask "Is this Karan?", or leave as Unknown. A wrong name is worse than Unknown. A speaker heard on both the microphone and Mac audio (`mixed`) is never auto-labelled.
- Starting thresholds (untested guesses to tune on real recordings): auto-label at cosine similarity 0.55 or more and 0.10 clear of the runner-up; ask between 0.40 and 0.55; need about 10 s of clean speech to create a profile, 5 s to add a sample or a negative to an existing person, and 3 s to identify.
- Live text while recording is display-only and uses `FluidLiveRecording.liveConfig`: a 2 s step with 1 s of lookahead (first words at about 3 s, then every 2 s; measured on a Mac, not yet on an iPhone 15). Do not use FluidAudio's `.streaming` preset: it decodes 11 s at a time, so the first words take 13 s. Each step is one model run, so check battery and heat on the iPhone before going below 2 s.
- Parakeet and community-1 are CC-BY-4.0, Nemotron 3 is OpenMDW-1.1; the app redistributes all three, so the attributions screen keeps their licences.

Measured on the real iPhone 15 (2026-11-25): Parakeet TDT v3 loads on its A16 chip (a different Parakeet variant fails there), and the setup times above; the setup after an app update is as long as the first, since iOS drops the compiled models on every install. Still to check there: peak memory and speed of the offline diariser and of CPU Nemotron on real recordings (no published figures exist), battery and heat, speaker labels on real speech, and the install and download size with the bundled models.

- **Order** (`publish-mac.sh`): (1) upload the DMG and read it back (size, sha256, signature against the committed key), then the delta the same way, and check the delta through the public site (so a Worker without the delta route stops the release before anything public changes); (2) write `latest.json` and check it through the public site (the bytes, "Version V ·" on the page, the 302 of `/transcribe/download`, the sha256 of the followed download), else put back the previous one and exit 1; (3) write `appcast.xml` and check the public bytes and signature, else put back the previous one; (4) delete every other `transcribe/Transcribe-*.dmg` and `transcribe/Transcribe-*.delta` and check the final set. A resume (and `--resume-check`) verifies the deltas `latest.json` lists as well as the DMG. Nothing public changes before step 2. ## Open decisions

- Voice sample groups: the join value 0.65 (tune on real recordings); it now drives auto-labelling and curation as well as the display, so tuning it matters more. Whether to group across microphones instead of within each one.
- One-sample sound groups score on their own (rule S2, decision D3 of 2026-11-26), with the symmetric runner-up (the best of the cohort and, for every other profile, the larger of its whole score W and its best group score G) and `negativeMax < A` for auto. The S1 value (a one-sample group borrowing its second place from the rest) is logged as `singletonBorrowShadow` in trace v3 for comparison; switch only after reading real numbers. Measured on synthetic data only (`GroupScoringSimulationTests`, 2026-11-26), sibling false-auto rates with no runner-up, today (the matcher before groups, which never holds more than 12 samples per channel) → S2: independent cosines N(0.38, 0.07), 20,000 trials: 1 group of 12: 1.45% → 1.45%; 3 groups of 4: 1.45% → 0.54%; 11 + 1 singleton: 1.45% → 2.03%; 12 singletons: 1.45% → 8.87%; 20 singletons (the ceiling): 1.65% (today's 12 of them) → 14.61%, about 9×, from S2 and the ceiling of 20 together (S2 alone at 12 samples is about 6×; the groupless score over all 20, a library only the ceiling makes, would be 3.67%). A vector model with sound environments (128-d, sibling about 0.40 in the same environment, 1,000 trials, today scored on the first 12 samples; `TRANSCRIBE_SIMULATIONS=full`): one environment 3.3% → 3.4%, 3 × 4 1.8% → 1.8%, 11 + 1 3.0% → 3.0%, 12 × 1 0.0% → 0.5%, 20 × 1 0.1% → 0.8%; with the sibling named (2 samples) 0% either way; the person's own probe auto-labels 99.3–100% either way, except a probe in the 20th environment, which today's 12 do not hold (97.7% → 100%). The default `swift test` run covers one environment and 12 × 1 with the sibling unnamed and named (200 trials, fixed seeds) and bounds the rates loosely; the named sibling there wins its own probe outright, so the symmetric runner-up is pinned by `GroupScoringTests.theRunnerUpIsSymmetric` and `theRunnerUpIsSymmetricOverRandomScores`. Three earlier unnamed recordings of the sibling in the cohort did not help (they score at least 0.40 against the person, so the cohort filter drops them). The risk sits with people whose samples are many one-sample groups, which curation now keeps up to 20 per channel.

  - The iPhone's automatic download window: the last 30 days, older audio when a recording is opened. Alternative: everything, like the Mac.
  - Existing sync users after the update: the backlog uploads by itself and a one-time Library card says so (decided). Still open: whether a large backlog (say over 1 GB) should wait for an explicit OK. Default: it starts at once.
  - Bit rate: AAC-LC 32 kbit/s (about 14.4 MB an hour). Alternative: 24 kbit/s (about 10.8 MB an hour) if speech still sounds fine; decide after real use.
  - Version: the change bumps no version; Liam bumps both apps to 0.3.4 after the Production schema deploy and the two-device check.
  - A full iCloud caused by audio: no in-app "remove audio from iCloud, keep transcripts" action in this version; the whole-sync pause says how much synced audio uses, and the user frees space or removes the iCloud copy. A real action needs a cross-device "audio removed" setting first, or the devices holding the audio upload it all again.
  - Debug builds with the real engine start with Sync audio off. Alternative: on, as in Release, with the two-device check always run in a second macOS user.
  - Records an older build dropped (the general case): not fixed. The `vg.` labels a 0.3.2 build dropped are still lost to it; audio uses the launch probe instead. Alternative: a one-time zone refetch through the catch-up path when the list of record types grows (touches `CloudKitTransport` and the core applier).
  - Long-lived CloudKit operations, if iPhone transfers turn out to be cut off when the app leaves the front.
  - Audio push latency: there is no separate push for audio; markers ride the record sync, so audio reaches a device as soon as its pushes (or its 60 s check in front) bring the marker, and pushes to the Mac were late or missing in Development. Revisit after real use.
- Updates: deltas from more than one earlier version (today only from the version before); a grace period before the old DMG is deleted; signed-feed key rotation tooling; running the Actions runner as a separate macOS user without the Wrangler, AWS and App Store Connect credentials (any approved workflow on it runs as Liam with the Developer ID key).
- Claude access: whether the helper should check who holds a well-known port before pairing there (follow-up from the 2026-11-25 pairing review, not built). The helper is unsandboxed, so it could find the process listening on `127.0.0.1:<port>` among its own user's processes (`proc_listpids` and `proc_pidfdinfo`; another user's sockets are not visible to it, so such a port would be skipped) and check that process's code signature (`SecCodeCopyGuestWithAttributes` with its pid, against a requirement for identifier `ai.w2solutions.transcribe.mac` and team `53FBF5CS9U`). That would stop another user's Transcribe, or any other listener on an earlier port, from capturing a pairing (accepted risk 2). Debug and UI-test builds signed to run locally would need their own requirement.
- Global shortcuts and dictation: a second binding per action for keyboards without fn; a login item (`SMAppService.mainApp`), since shortcuts work only while Transcribe is open; start and stop sounds; dictation during a recording; the clipboard restore delay (1.5 s); a trailing space after pasted text.
